# Slide-by-slide consistency audit: Stages 2 and 3C

Your concern is justified. Most of the slides are numerically consistent, but several plot different statistics, and there is one particularly important difference around Easting 176 that deserves explanation.

I checked the revised Beamer presentation, its figures, and the Stage 2 and Stage 3C CSV outputs.

The main source of confusion is that the presentation alternates between three quantities:

\\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Absolute GCV:}\quad &GCV_e(\lambda),\\\ \text{Relative GCV:}\quad &GCV_e(\lambda)-GCV\_{e,\infty},\\\ \text{Structural gap:}\quad &GCV\_{e,\text{best interior}}-GCV\_{e,\infty}. \end{aligned} \\]

These do not have to show the same numerical values, even when they come from the same Easting and spline model.

## 7. What we can say after the audit

Three findings stand out.

First, the original Eastings 214, 216, and 218 align numerically with the full-field work. Their GCV profiles, candidate scores, and treatment coefficients are consistent within each corresponding model specification.

Second, the manual and `mgcv` analyses are broadly consistent, but not identical. Their exact linear limits agree across all 199 Eastings, whereas the finite-smoothing results can differ. The near-tie at Easting 176 is the most consequential example.

Third, the diagrams currently make it too easy to confuse absolute GCV, GCV relative to linear, and optimizer GCV gaps.

My recommendation is to revise the presentation around a common set of definitions and highlight Eastings 80, 176, 214, 216, and 374 as explanatory examples.

The research result at 176 is particularly important: it suggests that small differences in the GCV criterion can change the preferred smoothing regime, with potentially substantial consequences for an unpenalized coefficient.

That is precisely the kind of phenomenon our later stationary-point and coefficient-sensitivity investigations should examine.
